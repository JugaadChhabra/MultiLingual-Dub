Three approaches, in the macOS language

No webfonts — this is SF Pro via -apple-system, which is most of why Mac apps feel like Mac apps. Every specimen follows your system appearance: switch your Mac between Light and Dark and this page follows, because that is what Apple-native actually means rather than picking a mood.

Each one shows the three things you asked for in place — a named error, an honest progress block, and the completion notification.

A  System Settings — sidebar + detail, both pipelines in one app

The sidebar holds Audio and Video, so your sound engineer and video editor open the same app and go to their own section. Recent jobs live there too, which is where "did last night's batch finish?" gets answered. When a job is running the detail pane becomes the progress view instead of keeping setup on screen forever.

C  Task sheet — setup, then a sheet owns the window

Setup is an ordinary form. Starting a run drops a macOS sheet over it that owns the window until the job ends — the same pattern as a system install or export. Smallest build and it puts progress dead centre, but there is nowhere for job history to live.

The completion notification — identical in all three

A real macOS notification plus a sound. It answers both halves of what you asked: finished and uploaded, and where it landed. The failure case names the cause in the notification itself, so they do not have to come back to the tab to find out what went wrong.

My recommendation

A, with C's behaviour inside it — sidebar and materials give the Mac-app shell, and when a job starts the detail pane becomes the progress view rather than keeping setup on screen. It is the only one of the three that solves a problem you have not named: two operators, two URLs, two diverging designs that I have to fix twice every time. One app with two sidebar sections means one design system, one status idiom, one notification path.

B is the safe choice if you would rather not merge the two pipelines. C is the cheapest and I would not pick it — losing job history costs more than the sheet is worth.

All three assume: no background animation, colour only for signal, and the four extra numbers already in JobSummary finally on screen — uploaded, rendered, failed, and time remaining derived from started_at.
